How this works

What the numbers mean

The headline metric is specific yield — kilowatt-hours generated per kilowatt-peak installed, written kWh/kWp. It is the only way to compare a 2 kWp array on a Galway bungalow with a 12 kWp array on a Wicklow barn. A bucket's figure is the total energy reported divided by the total capacity behind it, so each system counts in proportion to its size.

Alongside it we publish the median, computed in the database with percentile_cont. Where the mean and the median diverge sharply, a few unusual systems are pulling the average and the median is the more honest number.

Two different units appear on the site and it matters which is which. kWh/kWp per month is what almost every table shows: total energy over total capacity across whatever months are in the selection. kWh/kWp per year is computed only from systems that filed all twelve months of a given year — never by multiplying a monthly average by twelve, which would badly flatter anyone who only recorded the summer. {% if annual.year %} For {{ annual.year }} that annual figure rests on {{ annual.systems }} complete system-year{{ annual.systems|pluralize }}. {% endif %}

Raw kWh totals are shown too, but they mostly measure how many people from a county happened to sign up. They are not county-wide generation figures and should not be read as such.

What is published, and what never is

An aggregate appears only when at least {{ min_group_size }} distinct systems belonging to {{ min_group_size }} distinct contributors sit behind it. Below that threshold the bucket is listed as withheld, with no figure. This stops a county with one solar owner from effectively publishing that household's monthly electricity profile.

Never published, anywhere on the public site:

  • Handles or any account detail — and there are no email addresses or names to publish in the first place.
  • An individual system's readings, or anything that resolves to one system.
  • Anything finer-grained than a county — no addresses, no Eircodes, no coordinates.

Stated plainly: threshold suppression defeats casual re-identification. It is not a formal privacy guarantee, and someone who diffs snapshots of this site over time as contributors join could narrow things down. If that matters to you, do not contribute.

Why there is a login at all

It sits awkwardly with the word "anonymous", so it is worth being direct: the account exists so you can correct or delete your own figures later, and so a single person cannot stuff the dataset. But it is built to hold nothing about you.

You do not choose a username. Given the chance, people type a name they already use elsewhere, and that one field would tie a household to its electricity profile. The site issues a handle instead — two words and four digits, like bright-heron-4712 — generated at random and derived from nothing about you. There is no email field at all.

Your password is stored as an Argon2id hash, the memory-hard algorithm that won the Password Hashing Competition, never as the password itself.

The consequence is worth stating plainly: because we hold no way to contact or identify you, there is no password reset. Lose the handle or the password and access is gone permanently, for you and for us. Readings you already contributed stay in the published aggregates with no way for you to change or withdraw them. While you do have access, you can export everything you have contributed, or delete the account and every reading with it, from your own dashboard.

Readings we exclude

A monthly figure implying more than 250 kWh per kWp is physically impossible in Ireland — the whole country manages roughly 800–1,100 kWh/kWp in a year. Such rows are almost always watt-hours entered as kilowatt-hours, or a mistyped array size, and one of them can distort a county average badly. They stay in your own records but are excluded from every published aggregate.
